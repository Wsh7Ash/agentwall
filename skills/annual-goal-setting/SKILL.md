# Annual Goal Setting

> Set a year's hunting goals from last year's measured baselines with few, numeric and revisitable targets, so direction survives the drift of month-to-month session reality

**Use when:** the year is turning and you want direction grounded in measured performance rather than ambition

**Avoid when:** a major life change makes annual commitments unrealistic and quarterly focus is wiser

## Procedure

1. Establish baselines from measured history: acceptance rate, income per hour, programs active, skills used.
2. Set a small number of numeric goals, each with one number and one date, so nothing hides inside prose.
3. Attach a leading indicator you can act on monthly, since lagging metrics only inform you after the fact.
4. Write the rationale for each goal, connecting it to a gap or concentration the retrospectives revealed.
5. Schedule quarterly checkpoints that can revise the goals rather than merely report on them.
6. Close the year by scoring each goal honestly and recording what the gap teaches about your planning.

## Signals

- Goals are phrased as wishes like hunt more or earn better, which no checkpoint can score.
- More than a handful of goals exist, which means none of them will guide a real trade-off.
- Baselines were invented instead of measured, so the targets are anchored to hope.
- No quarterly checkpoint exists, so drift goes uncorrected until the annual review.

## Patterns

**1. Goal line**

```text
goal=raise-dup-rate-from-0.24-to-0.15 date=2026-12-31 leading=angle-check-per-queue-item review=quarterly
```

**2. Baseline note**

```text
metric=ev-per-hour base=95 from=2026-h2 basis=measured-not-estimated confidence=medium
```

**3. Checkpoint**

```text
q1=on-track q2=off-action=narrow-programs q3=on-track q4=final-score=recorded-and-explained
```

## Pitfalls

- Setting goals in areas with no measurement, which guarantees they cannot be scored.
- Writing so many goals that trade-offs never force a choice, which is the whole purpose of a goal.
- Anchoring to aspiration instead of to the measured baseline, which produces numbers nobody believes.
- Treating checkpoints as reporting rather than as permission to revise the goal itself.

## Done when

- Each goal carries one numeric target, one date and a baseline measured from real history.
- A leading indicator exists for each goal that can be acted on monthly.
- Quarterly checkpoints are scheduled with explicit authority to revise goals.
