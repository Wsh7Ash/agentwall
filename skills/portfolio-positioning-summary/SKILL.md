# Portfolio Positioning Summary

> Maintain a short public summary of what you actually find well, so programs, collaborators and future engagements can route interesting work to you without guesswork

**Use when:** you have a body of accepted work and want the right opportunities to find you

**Avoid when:** your accepted record is too thin for any honest positioning claim yet

## Procedure

1. Read your accepted reports and note the classes where you consistently deliver rather than occasionally land.
2. Draft positioning claims only from accepted work, since unaccepted claims are indefensible under scrutiny.
3. State the kinds of targets and programs you engage well, which helps others judge fit before contacting you.
4. Keep the summary short enough to read in one sitting and specific enough to be actionable.
5. Update it when accepted work changes the picture, keeping a dated history rather than overwriting silently.
6. Pair it with how to reach you through the channels you actually monitor.

## Signals

- Your accepted work clusters clearly but your public summary does not say so, so opportunities route elsewhere.
- Draft claims include work under review, which risks being cited back to you if it is declined.
- The summary lists every class you have touched, which communicates nothing about where you are strong.
- Contact channels listed are ones you no longer monitor.

## Patterns

**1. Claim line**

```text
supported-by=6-accepted class=tenant-isolation claim=depth-not-breadth evidence=links-on-request
```

**2. Fit note**

```text
targets=multi-tenant-saas engagement=api-heavy avoids=no-hardware note=helps-routing
```

**3. Position version**

```text
updated=2026-10-27 claims=accepted-only pending-excluded contact=platform-primary review=quarterly
```

## Pitfalls

- Claiming breadth across classes where your record shows one real strength.
- Including pending or rejected work as evidence, which collapses if anyone checks.
- Publishing contact routes you have abandoned, so the positioning works and the response never comes.
- Never updating it, so the summary describes a practitioner you stopped being a year ago.

## Done when

- Every claim is backed by accepted work you can point to when asked.
- The summary names the target fit that helps others route work correctly.
- Contact channels listed are monitored, and the summary carries a review date.
